\section{Abstract}

This thesis studies finite-horizon price tracking in a synthetic, zero-fee constant-product automated market maker (AMM) after a shock to an exogenous centralized-exchange (CEX) reference. It separates exact swap mechanics from effects of arbitrage capacity, reserve depth, agent rules and event order. In an isolated pool, capacity $c$ corrects out-of-band log error by at most $2\log(1+c)$ per tick; a 0.20 log shock with a 0.003 band therefore needs four ideal ticks at $c=0.03$. The reproducible study evaluates 13,500 main paths, a 40,500-path sensitivity grid and a 9,000-path replay of archived seeds.

High-capacity arbitrage reduces mean cumulative excess-error area from 2.36 without arbitrage to 0.10, while the direction of that contrast is built into the rule. The one-shot withdrawal trigger fires in every E3 path. In the shallow replay, E3 later departures occur in 98.2--99.6\,\% of recovered paths and often recur; a fixed-capacity control equalizes the configured pressure ratios with static E1 and reduces the post-recovery contrast from 0.122--0.124 to 0.0036--0.0097. A targeted 900-path fixed-capacity/no-panic check yields zero later departures in every shock cell, supporting $R$ as a candidate organizing hypothesis rather than a validated law. The control is post hoc, covers one depth and conditions on recovery, so it does not identify separate causal effects of capacity and depth.

The two-state reduced map improves on persistence in some held-out conditions but not on scalar AR(1). A band-aware hybrid map lowers recursive replay error by about 1.7\,\%, using the realized future CEX path; it is therefore not a real-time forecast. Results remain conditional on the synthetic rule set and lack market-data validation. The contribution is a reproducible mechanism study that identifies testable telemetry and replay requirements for future work with synchronized prices, realistic fees, delays, trading costs, finite capital and competing arbitrageurs.

\textbf{Keywords:} automated market maker; arbitrage; price tracking; liquidity withdrawal; agent-based simulation; Monte Carlo
