\section{Introduction}

Decentralized exchanges based on automated market makers (AMMs) and centralized
exchanges (CEXs) represent complementary price-formation mechanisms. Their
interaction through arbitrage and differences in liquidity makes it possible to study
price tracking and responses to shocks. The intended contribution is not a general
theory of market stability, but a set of mechanisms that can be verified in a
simulator: how much correction comes from arbitrage, how it changes under limited
capacity, and how liquidity depth changes the impact of a fixed-size sale. The reduced
model is subordinate to this computational evidence and is not presented as an
automatic equivalent of a real market.

\section{Problem, research question, and hypotheses}

\subsection{Scientific problem}

For the proposed minimal model, it is not yet sufficiently determined under which
combinations of arbitrage capacity, liquidity depth, execution frictions, and agent
behavior price tracking is achieved, a discrepancy persists, or a disturbance is
amplified. A simulation that only plots these effects does not by itself explain the
conditions that produce them.

\subsection{Main research question}

Under what conditions of arbitrage, liquidity, and heterogeneous behavior does a
simplified hybrid CEX/DEX market reduce or maintain bounded tracking error, and to
what extent does a reduced discrete map describe the regimes observed in a stochastic
multi-agent microworld?

\subsection{Working hypotheses}

Price tracking depends on the balance between corrective feedback (arbitrage) and
potentially amplifying mechanisms (trading pressure and adaptive liquidity). Variation
in these mechanisms produces behavior regions that can be distinguished with
experimental metrics; the reduced map serves as a local diagnostic and must fail
explicitly when its assumptions are no longer reasonable.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[H1a.] Under specified assumptions about capacity, latency, and costs,
    arbitrage reduces discrepancies after an isolated disturbance.
  \item[H1b.] Proportional liquidity withdrawal increases the impact of a subsequent
    fixed-size trade; certain adaptive LP rules amplify shocks compared with a static
    liquidity counterfactual.
  \item[H1c.] A discrete map estimated or diagnosed around an operating region can
    partially classify error contraction or persistence in the ABM; disagreements are
    outcomes that delimit its validity.
\end{description}

\section{Objectives}

\textbf{General objective.} Develop and evaluate a computational framework for
analyzing CEX/DEX price tracking and liquidity fragility by combining a traceable
reduced dynamic map with heterogeneous-agent simulation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Specify the state, AMM transaction mechanics, arbitrage, and author-approved
    four-role policies using the ODD protocol \cite{grimm2020odd}.
  \item Construct a reduced discrete map with a no-arbitrage band and variables
    observable in the ABM; evaluate local contraction and linearized stability only in
    regions where those criteria apply.
  \item Implement and verify a reproducible simulator with explicit reserves,
    inventories, costs, and event ordering.
  \item Design factorial or sensitivity experiments for shocks, arbitrage intensity,
    depth, and LP response.
  \item Compare theoretical and simulated results, including disagreement regions,
    statistical robustness, and limits to extrapolation.
\end{enumerate}
