\section{Abstract}

Hybrid crypto markets combine centralized trading mechanisms with decentralized
automated market makers (AMMs). Arbitrage can reduce price discrepancies, while
execution constraints, heterogeneous trading decisions, and endogenous liquidity
changes can alter adjustment dynamics. This thesis proposes a two-level framework:
(i) a reduced discrete map with simulator-observable variables; and (ii) a stochastic
microworld with an external CEX reference, a constant-product AMM, and four author-approved representative roles. The reproducible MVP has deterministic mechanism checks and a
300-seed paired selection model. In its configured scenarios, higher arbitrage capacity
reduces peak tracking error, while the adaptive-LP effect changes with shock direction.
On held-out seeds and shock magnitudes, the estimated map's Schur-stability label
matches the majority contraction outcome in only 3 of 8 groups. These synthetic
selection results do not validate the model against real markets or make the map
coefficients empirical parameters. Broader agent-design review and confirmation
experiments remain necessary.

\textbf{Keywords:} CEX/DEX price tracking; AMM; arbitrage; adaptive liquidity;
multi-agent simulation; discrete map; reproducibility.
