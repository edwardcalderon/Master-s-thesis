\section*{Glossary of technical terms}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Glossary of technical terms}

\begingroup
\small
\setlength{\parskip}{0.15em}
The definitions below describe how each term is used in this thesis. They refer to
the specified simulator unless they explicitly state a general mathematical property.

\subsection*{Markets and agents}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,labelwidth=0pt,labelsep=0.5em,itemsep=0.1em]
  \item[ABM --- agent-based model:] A computational model in which individually represented agents follow specified rules, interact with one another and with an environment, and collectively generate the simulated outcomes. Here, the agents are noise traders, a trend/panic trader, an arbitrageur, and a liquidity provider.
  \item[Agent heterogeneity:] Differences in agents' roles, information, rules, inventories, or actions. In this study, heterogeneity comes from the distinct trading and liquidity-provision rules; it does not imply that the agents are calibrated representations of real participants.
  \item[AMM --- automated market maker:] A decentralized trading mechanism that quotes prices and executes swaps according to a reserve-based rule rather than matching orders in a conventional order book.
  \item[CEX --- centralized exchange:] A trading venue represented here only by an exogenous reference-price series. Its order book, market impact, and feedback with the simulated AMM are outside the model.
  \item[DEX --- decentralized exchange:] A trading venue implemented through blockchain-based protocols. In this thesis, the DEX side is represented by the specified AMM pool.
  \item[CPMM --- constant-product market maker:] An AMM whose ideal, fee-free swap rule keeps the product of two reserves, $xy=k$, constant. Fees and liquidity additions or withdrawals can change that product; the equality is not an unconditional invariant of every simulated event.
  \item[Liquidity pool:] The assets held by an AMM and made available for swaps. In this model it contains the two reserves $x_t$ and $y_t$.
  \item[Liquidity provider (LP):] An agent that supplies assets to an AMM pool. The simulated adaptive LP follows a stylized withdrawal rule; it is not an empirically estimated model of provider behavior.
  \item[Inventory:] The quantity of each asset held by an agent. Inventory limits which simulated orders an agent can execute.
  \item[Noise trader:] An agent whose order side and timing are generated randomly rather than from a price signal or information about value.
  \item[Trend/panic trader:] The simulated agent that follows a recent AMM price movement or, after a specified negative-return threshold, submits a panic sale.
  \item[Order flow:] The sequence and sizes of buy and sell orders arriving at the pool. Paired runs reuse the same pregenerated noise orders and reference shocks.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Market mechanics and price response}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,labelwidth=0pt,labelsep=0.5em,itemsep=0.1em]
  \item[Arbitrage:] Trading across venues to benefit from a price discrepancy. In the simulator, the arbitrageur trades only when the AMM-reference discrepancy is outside the specified band and its order is subject to a capacity limit.
  \item[Arbitrage capacity:] The maximum fraction of the tradable reserve that the simulated arbitrageur may trade in one tick. It is a controlled model parameter, not a measured real-world capacity.
  \item[No-arbitrage band:] The interval around the reference price in which the model does not activate arbitrage. It represents an operating threshold; it is not estimated from observed transaction costs.
  \item[Liquidity and depth:] The reserves available to absorb a swap. In this constant-product pool, reducing reserves increases the price impact of a given absolute order. This definition is specific to the model's two-asset pool.
  \item[Swap:] An exchange of one pool asset for the other under the AMM's reserve rule.
  \item[Slippage:] The difference between a swap's average execution price and the pool's marginal price immediately before the swap. It depends on order size relative to reserves and, when included, fees.
  \item[Price impact:] The change in the pool's marginal price caused by a swap. It is related to, but not identical to, slippage.
  \item[Reference price:] The exogenous CEX price used as the comparison target. It does not respond to simulated AMM trades.
  \item[Exogenous shock:] A prescribed change applied to the reference price from outside the model. The confirmatory design uses a one-time shock so the AMM response can be compared under controlled conditions.
  \item[Tracking error:] The logarithmic difference between the AMM marginal price and the reference price, $e_t=\log(p^{\mathrm D}_t/p^{\mathrm C}_t)$. Its sign identifies which price is higher.
  \item[Excess error:] The part of the tracking error outside the no-arbitrage band, represented by $d_t$. Its absolute value is zero inside the band and measures the discrepancy that remains eligible for correction outside it.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Dynamical description and experiments}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,labelwidth=0pt,labelsep=0.5em,itemsep=0.1em]
  \item[State:] The quantities used to summarize a system at a given tick. The simulator's full state includes reserves, prices, and agent inventories; the reduced map retains only excess error and net buying pressure.
  \item[Micro-world:] A deliberately bounded computational environment with explicit agents, rules, and interaction mechanisms. Its findings concern the specified model configurations, not a complete representation of a real market.
  \item[Affine map:] A rule of the form $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+b$, with a linear transformation and a constant offset. The fitted reduced map has this form for each stated condition.
  \item[Reduced map:] The estimated two-state affine discrete-time rule used to describe next-tick excess error and buying pressure. It is a reduced description of the simulator, not an exact replacement for its full nonlinear, rule-based dynamics.
  \item[Local model / linearization:] A simplified dynamical description intended to apply near a stated operating condition or within a stated regime. Results from a local model do not establish behavior outside that domain.
  \item[Markov property:] A property under which the next state depends on the current state, without requiring earlier states, once the model inputs are given. The two-variable reduced map is not assumed to have this property for the full simulator.
  \item[Regime:] A category used to describe a pattern of simulated tracking behavior. The thesis labels trajectories as in-band, contractive, or persistent using the finite-window rules stated in the methods; these labels are not claims of global dynamical stability.
  \item[Schur stability:] Stability of an autonomous discrete-time linear map when all eigenvalues of its matrix have magnitude strictly below one (equivalently, its spectral radius is below one). The thesis applies this criterion to the fitted reduced map; it does not prove global stability of the full stochastic simulator.
  \item[Tick:] One discrete update step of the simulation. At each tick, the model applies the prescribed shock when scheduled, processes agent actions in a fixed order, and records the resulting state.
  \item[Seed:] The initial value for a pseudorandom-number generator. Runs with different seeds provide different stochastic realizations; the seed is the independent unit used for the confirmatory contrasts.
  \item[Paired design:] A comparison in which two conditions share the same seed and exogenous inputs, so their within-seed difference isolates the changed model rule under common simulated conditions.
  \item[Exploration and confirmation:] Separate simulation sets. Exploration is used to estimate the reduced map and plan precision; confirmation evaluates prespecified hypotheses on disjoint seeds and designated cells.
  \item[Held-out configuration:] A parameter combination withheld from fitting and used to assess whether the reduced map describes outcomes beyond the configurations used to estimate it.
  \item[H1--H3:] The three prespecified hypotheses: H1 concerns arbitrage and excess-error area; H2 concerns adaptive liquidity withdrawal and maximum price response; H3 compares the reduced map's one-step prediction error with persistence.
  \item[E0--E3:] Labels used in the experimental-design table for the reference, arbitrage, depth-sensitivity, and adaptive-withdrawal cases. E2 is an analytical depth control, not an independently simulated condition. The labels are not general market categories.
  \item[OE1--OE4:] Labels for the four specific objectives stated in the objectives chapter.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Inference and scope}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed,leftmargin=0pt,labelwidth=0pt,labelsep=0.5em,itemsep=0.1em]
  \item[MAE --- mean absolute error:] The average absolute difference between predicted and observed next-tick values. Here it is computed over one-step predictions of excess error; lower MAE indicates smaller average absolute prediction error.
  \item[Persistence benchmark:] A simple prediction that carries the current observed value forward, adjusted for the known reference-price input. It tests whether the reduced map improves on a minimal baseline.
  \item[Scalar autoregression:] A one-variable prediction rule in which the next value depends linearly on the current value. It is a comparison model for assessing the contribution of the reduced map's second state.
  \item[Sign-flip randomization test:] A paired inference procedure that compares the observed mean contrast with contrasts formed by randomly reversing the signs of the per-seed differences under the null hypothesis.
  \item[Percentile bootstrap confidence interval:] An interval formed from quantiles of repeated resamples of the independent per-seed contrasts. It quantifies sampling uncertainty under the stated resampling design.
  \item[Confidence interval:] A range calculated by a stated procedure to express uncertainty in an estimated quantity. The thesis reports 95\,\% intervals for paired contrasts.
  \item[Holm correction:] A stepwise adjustment of multiple-test p-values that controls the family-wise probability of one or more false rejections across the specified family of hypotheses.
  \item[Verification:] Checking that the implementation follows its formal or stated specification, for example by testing conservation identities, bounds, and event rules.
  \item[Validation:] Assessing whether the model is adequate for its intended target. The thesis verifies implementation properties but does not validate the model against market data.
  \item[External validity:] The extent to which findings apply beyond the simulated configurations to other settings, such as actual exchanges or production systems. The results here are synthetic and do not establish external validity.
\end{description}
