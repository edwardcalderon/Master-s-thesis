\section{Scope of the results}

This chapter reports analytical identities, Monte Carlo comparisons over 13\,500 simulator runs, an engineering sensitivity over 40\,500 paths (section~\ref{sec:engineering-sensitivity}), and a held-out comparison of affine and hybrid reduced maps over 9\,600 paths (section~\ref{sec:hybrid-results}). \subsection{Ideal correction rate under bounded arbitrage}

In the absence of other orders and with a fixed reference, a capacity-limited swap of $q=cx$ changes the log price by $\delta_c=2\log(1+c)$. If the error is outside the band, the remaining excess follows $D_{t+1}=\max(0,D_t-\delta_c)$, and the ideal number of ticks to reach the band is
\[
  N_\gamma=\left\lceil\frac{D_0}{2\log(1+c)}\right\rceil,\qquad D_0=(|e_0|-\gamma)_+.
\]
For a log shock of magnitude $0.20$ and $\gamma=0.003$, the ideal counts are 20 ticks at $c=0.005$, four at $c=0.03$, and two at $c=0.10$; with $c=0$, correction does not occur. The low-capacity result is therefore largely implied by the rule and the 20-tick horizon. Simulation adds information about how noise, trend, withdrawal and event ordering change that benchmark.

\section{H1: simulated correction against the analytical benchmark}
\label{sec:res-h1}

The ideal calculation predicts 20 ticks to the band at the low capacity $c=0.005$, the full simulation horizon; the high-capacity rule $c=0.03$ needs only four ticks before other agents act. In the held-out runs, the mean excess area is $2.363$ in E0, $1.056$ in E1-low and $0.100$ in E1-high. \section{H2: withdrawal and order-scale sensitivity}
\label{sec:res-h2}

Under the legacy statistic $M=\max_t|\log(p_t^D/p_0^D)|$, the paired E3-minus-E1-high difference is $+0.0114$ on average (95\,\% seed-bootstrap interval $[0.0107,0.0121]$). This statistic measures displacement from the pre-shock AMM price, not overshoot or maximum tracking error relative to the new CEX reference. It also saturates at $|\text{shock}|+\gamma$ in six of nine cells. It is retained only as a description of the original design. The later whole-horizon peak $P=\max_{0\le t\le20}|e_t|$ has a related limitation: it includes tick 1, when the imposed shock dominates both conditions, so its contrast shrinks as shock size grows.

The per-cell differences in table~\ref{tab:celdas} are positive only at depth factor $\kappa=0.5$ and zero at $\kappa=1$ and $2$. The withdrawal activates in all E3 paths because every applied shock exceeds the fixed trigger $|e_t|>0.02$; it usually occurs at tick 1. This is a deterministic one-time intervention under the tested shocks, not a test of heterogeneous or gradual provider responses. The panic order is activated only at $\kappa=0.5$, where the configured noise-order impact is $1.6\,\%$, just 0.1 percentage point beyond the $1.5\,\%$ threshold.

We therefore ran a post-hoc replay of the archived seeds and moved the primary exploratory H2 diagnostic to the cumulative excess error after recovery is confirmed. Let $t_c$ be the second of the two consecutive in-band observations that define sustained return; then $A_{\mathrm{post}}=\sum_{t>t_c}(|e_t|-\gamma)_+$. At depth $\kappa=0.5$, the paired E3-minus-E1-high difference is $0.1229$ (95\,\% seed-bootstrap interval $[0.1092,0.1373]$, $n=267$), $0.1239$ ($[0.1134,0.1348]$, $n=272$), and $0.1215$ ($[0.1109,0.1326]$, $n=287$) for shocks $-0.05$, $-0.10$, and $-0.20$, respectively. At depths 1 and 2, the paired contrasts are zero to numerical precision in all shock cells. Figure~\ref{fig:h2-post-recovery} shows this depth pattern. The similar shallow-depth contrasts across shock sizes, after excluding the shock and the two confirmation ticks, are more directly relevant to post-recovery fragility than the old peak metric. This remains a conditional, post-hoc comparison among recovered paths, not a confirmatory or market-level effect.

The pressure-ratio counterexample can be resolved mechanistically within the tested design. At shallow depth and $c=0.03$, the continuous noise-plus-trend input gives $R_{\mathrm{flow}}=0.670$ in the static case, below one, while the configured one-off panic input gives $R_{\mathrm{panic}}=1.327$; the maximum same-direction noise-plus-panic input gives $R_{\max}^{\mathrm{adv}}=1.586$. At E3's nominal post-withdrawal reserve, these ratios are 1.208, 2.375 and 2.831 (table~\ref{tab:pressure-ratio}). The panic-disabled replay yields no later departures in E1-high static (0/300 at each shock; Wilson 95\,\% upper limit 1.3\,\%), whereas the panic-allowed departures are single episodes and all 143 overlap the panic-order tick. In E3, 98.2--99.6\,\% of recovered paths depart later, and about 92--95\,\% have multiple departure episodes; this pattern remains when panic is disabled. These results support a narrow mechanism statement: the E1 events in these shallow cells are panic-associated one-off departures, while repeated E3 departures do not require panic. They do not establish a universal pressure threshold; the ratios use configured orders and approximate reserves, not realized directional flow at each event.

We also ran a paired capacity-reference counterfactual at $\kappa=0.5$: the arbitrage cap was fixed at $c$ times the initial input reserve, with execution still capped by the pool's active input reserve. Across the three shocks, E3 recovery increased from 90.3--95.7\,\% under the original reserve-linked cap to 100\,\%; its later-departure share among all runs fell from 88.7--94.7\,\% to 17.7--41.0\,\%. The post-recovery E3-minus-static excess-area contrast fell from 0.122--0.124 to 0.0036--0.0097; the paired current-cap-minus-fixed-cap contrast was 0.114--0.118 with 95\,\% intervals excluding zero. This isolates a substantial role for the capacity coupling in this one shallow-depth design. A residual contrast remains, and interactions and recovery conditioning prevent interpreting the difference as a universal causal share. The broader withdrawal-by-capacity-by-depth factorial is still needed.

A broader follow-up should vary withdrawal, capacity reference, depth, order scale and panic independently in a frozen factorial design, with the primary post-recovery estimand and recovery coverage declared in advance. The present replay is exploratory and reuses existing seeds; it is not a new independent sample.

\section{H3: predictive comparison against simple baselines}
\label{sec:res-h3}

The map clause is answered here as a comparison of continuous-error predictive description on held-out simulator paths; the finite-horizon labels are descriptive summaries rather than observed class labels. The two-state map is fitted to signed excess error $d_t$, which is zero inside the band and changes slope at its boundary. A single affine relation cannot represent this threshold exactly. On held-out cells, the map has lower mean absolute error than persistence in most conditions, but does not outperform scalar AR(1) in any principal condition. Its second state therefore adds no demonstrated one-step predictive value in this design; the map remains a descriptive simulator summary rather than a regime model.

\section{Held-out comparison of affine and hybrid maps}
\label{sec:hybrid-results}

The new comparison uses analysis 0.1.0, run \code{\HybridRunID}, and signed price error. On 9\,600 held-out trajectories from 300 new seeds, the hybrid map reduced mean recursive price-error MAE from 0.030807 to 0.030291. The paired mean improvement was 0.000516 (95\,\% seed-bootstrap interval [0.000499, 0.000533]); 99.7\,\% of seed-averaged contrasts favored Hyb. Relative to L0, the average improvement is about 1.7\,\%. This is a small replay-error gain within the specified simulator; the evaluation receives the realized future CEX path and is not a real-time forecasting test.

Each condition contributed 12\,000 selection transitions. Both sides of the band were represented (table~\ref{tab:hybrid-regimes}), although the low-arbitrage model had fewer in-band observations. The fit remains condition-specific, and this count does not establish that the two-variable state captures all transition-relevant memory.

\section{Engineering sensitivity: recovery and order scale}
\label{sec:engineering-sensitivity}

A supplementary analysis (analysis 0.6.0, run \code{\EngineeringRunID}) used 300 new seeds (2000--2299), three order-flow variants and the 15 depth/shock/condition cells of the held-out grid: 40\,500 paths, 20 abstract ticks each. It is a descriptive and exploratory sensitivity analysis, not an inferential test. Recovery means the first observed pair of consecutive ticks inside the band; a later departure is counted separately. Deadlines are ticks, not elapsed time.

With the original fixed absolute order sizes, E3 threshold-withdrawal recovered at least once in 97.4\,\% of paths, yet only 81.9\,\% ended in-band. Of paths that recovered, 31.2\,\% later departed. At depth 0.5, only 45.8\,\% ended in-band and 98.8\,\% of recovered paths later departed. Thus a first sustained return is not equivalent to persistent tracking in this model. These equally weighted design averages carry no multiplicity-adjusted population claim.

The depth-specific threshold-withdrawal-minus-static maximum-response contrast changes with order definition (table~\ref{tab:engineering-sensitivity}). Holding absolute orders fixed produced an effect at depth 0.5, while scaling the noise and trend orders with initial reserves made the contrast effectively zero at all three depths. Disabling panic reduced, but did not eliminate, this shock-dominated whole-horizon metric. The post-recovery replay below separates that one-time event from subsequent departures. This sensitivity shows that depth alone does not determine the result; order size relative to reserves and the reaction rule matter.

\subsection{Pressure and departure mechanisms: exploratory replay}

The frozen exploratory replay protocol 0.7.0 reruns 9\,000 paths using the existing seeds 2000--2299: 5\,400 fixed-order paths over the three depths and shocks, 1\,800 shallow no-panic paths, and 1\,800 shallow paths with the arbitrage input cap fixed to its initial-reserve amount. All 7\,200 paths using the original reserve-linked cap reproduce their archived recovery, departure, excess-area, maximum-response and capital-limit metrics exactly. The replay is therefore a reproducibility and mechanism analysis of existing seeds, not an independent Monte Carlo sample.

For a configured input order $q$ and reserve $x$, define $R(q,x,c)=2\log(1+q/x)/\delta_c$, with $\delta_c=2\log(1+c)$. The conservative one-tick diagnostic is $R_{\mathrm{1tick}}=\max_{q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{adv}}}R(q,x,c)$, where $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{adv}}$ includes the largest adverse configured input, including panic. At depth 0.5 and capacity $c=0.03$, the decomposition reports $R_{\mathrm{flow}}$ for $q=100$ (noise plus trend aligned), $R_{\mathrm{panic}}$ for the one-off panic order $q=200$, and $R_{\mathrm{1tick}}=R_{\max}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ for $q=240$ (noise plus panic aligned; panic replaces rather than adds to trend). The post-withdrawal E3 reserve is approximated as 55\,\% of the starting reserve. These configured ratios are not realized tick-by-tick measurements; noise occurs with probability 0.7, event directions may differ, and the reserve changes within a tick.

\section{Summary of the results}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Analytical arbitrage benchmark:} an isolated pool with capacity $c$ reduces out-of-band log error by at most $2\log(1+c)$ per tick. The matched-deadline table shows how rarely a two-tick sustained return begins by the ideal first-entry deadline at low capacity; the direction of correction remains built into the rule.
  \item \textbf{Withdrawal sensitivity:} post-recovery excess area is higher under E3 only at depth 0.5 with fixed orders. A fixed-initial-cap counterfactual removes most of this contrast in that cell, but the result remains post-hoc and condition-specific.
  \item \textbf{Departure mechanism:} E1-high static later departures are single panic-associated episodes and disappear in the no-panic ablation. E3 has repeated departures in most recovered paths, including when panic is disabled. These are programmed-model outcomes, not market frequencies.
  \item \textbf{Pressure ratios:} configured continuous-flow ratios alone miss the E1 one-off panic event; the panic and maximum-adverse ratios exceed one. The ratios remain exploratory inputs and do not establish a threshold law.
  \item \textbf{Reduced models:} the two-state map improves on persistence in some conditions but does not beat scalar AR(1). The band-aware map's approximately 1.7\,\% replay-error gain is concentrated in one condition and uses the realized future CEX path.
\end{itemize}
