\section{Scope, risks, and exit criteria}

In scope: constant-product AMM, external CEX price, four agents, a traceable local
discrete map, E0--E3, reproducibility, synthetic experiments, and limited comparison
with market facts.

Out of scope: profitable forecasting, operational exchanges, full limit-order-book
replicas, blockchain consensus, advanced MEV, lending, bridges, reinforcement
learning, and general guarantees across all markets. For now, it is also out of scope
to claim empirical validity for the map based only on preliminary simulations. The
main risk is that the reduced map may not approximate the ABM outside a local region.
Mitigation: separate selection from confirmation, record configurations, compare
ablations, and report disagreement as a result. Other risks include overparameterization,
seed dependence, assumptions of unlimited liquidity, confusion between local and
global stability, and calibration bias.

\section{Discussion and limits to inference}

The analysis must distinguish three levels: mechanical properties derived from the
ideal AMM, local diagnostics from the reduced map, and patterns from a stochastic
discrete ABM that remain subject to validation. The map's Schur condition and
preliminary contraction do not guarantee stability in real markets or replace
evaluation with public data. The research must report the regimes in which the
approximation reduces error well and those in which it fails because of saturation,
capital limits, persistent shocks, or liquidity changes.

\section{Conclusions and future work}

The project is framed as a mechanism-centered experimental framework for price
tracking, bounded arbitrage, and liquidity fragility. Preliminary simulations show
that the prototype reproduces the expected comparative directions for E0--E3 and
allow a generic ODE to be replaced with a discrete map whose variables are observable.
The decisive phase remains: specify ex ante criteria, run separate sensitivity and
confirmation analyses, and evaluate external validity with a bounded set of public
data. None of the current results demonstrates usefulness in a real market.
