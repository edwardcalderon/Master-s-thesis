\section{Abstract}

Hybrid crypto markets combine centralized trading mechanisms with decentralized
automated market makers (AMMs). Arbitrage can reduce price discrepancies, while
execution constraints, heterogeneous trading decisions, and endogenous liquidity
changes can alter the adjustment dynamics. This thesis proposes a two-level
methodological framework: (i) a reduced discrete map whose variables are observable in
the simulator; and (ii) a stochastic microworld with an external CEX reference price, a
constant-product AMM, and four agent types. Preliminary synthetic simulations verify
the expected mechanical directions: arbitrage bounds tracking error, lower capacity
prolongs recovery, and adaptive liquidity withdrawal amplifies the impact of a fixed
sale. These checks do not validate the model against real markets or turn reduced-map
coefficients into empirical parameters. The research will compare the reduced map with
the ABM through controlled scenarios, sensitivity analysis, and reported disagreement.

\textbf{Keywords:} CEX/DEX price tracking; AMM; arbitrage; adaptive liquidity;
multi-agent simulation; discrete map; reproducibility.
